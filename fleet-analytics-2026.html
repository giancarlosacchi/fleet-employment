Timeline Table

1 · Tonnage coming open, month by month

Why: this is the re-chartering pipeline. Each bar = vessels reaching earliest redelivery that month (stacked by segment), i.e. when each ship can legally come back to the market and Diana starts looking for the next employment. The green bars add the two Kamsarmax newbuildings, which also need first employment. Clusters (Sep–Dec 2026, Jan–Mar 2027) are the windows to be in front of them with cargoes and period ideas — and they also tell you when Diana has pricing pressure from several open ships at once.

2 · Committed gross revenue run-rate

Why: the coverage cliff. Solid line = $/day locked in counting only minimum periods; dashed = if every charterer keeps the ship to the latest redelivery. The gap between the lines is charterers' optionality; the slope shows how fast cover burns off. By mid-2027 almost everything is open — that is where forward appetite (period, FFA-backed cover) matters most.

3 · Achieved rate vs deadweight

Why: a one-glance benchmark of what this owner actually fixed per size. Use it to price similar tonnage: e.g. modern Capes cluster at $24–27.5k while the older SEMIRIO took $21.65k; Ultras sit in a tight $12–16k band; ATALANDI's $10.1k and ISMENE's $11k show the discount on older LR/Greek-flag Pmaxes. Outliers above the cloud were fixed in stronger windows — worth checking the fixture date before quoting them.

4 · Charterer exposure

Why: who controls this fleet today. Bars = number of ships per charterer, line = combined gross $/day with that counterparty. Cargill (5), Bunge (4), SwissMarine (4) and NYK (4) hold ~45% of the fleet — they are the natural first call when a ship comes open (incumbent advantage), and also the names whose redeliveries to watch: if one of them turns seller of tonnage, several Diana ships hit the market together.

5 · Age profile by segment

Why: age drives what employment a ship can get. Each bar = vessel age today (red line = 15 years, where period interest thins out, surveys get expensive and scrubber/CII economics bite). MELIA and CALIPSO (21y) and SEMIRIO (19y) are realistic sale/demo candidates rather than period ships; the 2016–2022 units (FLORIDA, the DSI Ultras, the Newcastlemaxes) are the ones worth pitching for longer period.

6 · Drydock calendar (off-hire planning)

Why: every blue block is a ship out of the market ~3–4 weeks. Two uses: (a) when several dockings stack in one quarter (Q4 2026 has four), Diana's effective open fleet is smaller than the redelivery profile suggests — fewer ships to offer; (b) a ship docking right after redelivery (SAN FRANCISCO, MYRTO, MAIA, FLORIDA…) will want a short ballast-friendly last leg, which is a concrete angle when proposing the final voyage or the next employment with delivery ex-yard.
